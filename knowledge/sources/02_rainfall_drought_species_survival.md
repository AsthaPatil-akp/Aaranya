Darukaa.Earth Knowledge Synthesis Report 02
Rainfall, Drought, Soil Moisture and Species Survival
Document type: institutional scientific synthesis
Topics: climate, water_availability, drought, species_survival

1. Purpose
This report explains how rainfall pattern, drought, soil moisture, and temperature jointly influence vegetation health and species survival. It is intended for multi-metric environmental reasoning rather than single-factor climate commentary.

2. Water as a biodiversity constraint
Species survival in terrestrial ecosystems is tightly linked to water availability. Plants, soil fauna, and many terrestrial invertebrates experience climate stress first as water stress. IPCC climate assessments describe increasing drought risk in many dryland agricultural regions, with knock-on effects for ecosystem productivity and land degradation.

Low rainfall is not automatically lethal. Harm increases when low rainfall coincides with low soil moisture storage, high evaporative demand, and little vegetation shade. Semi-arid farms with bare soil after harvest lose a larger share of each rainfall event to evaporation.

3. Drought plus heat
High temperature raises vapour pressure deficit. Vegetation with sparse canopy then heats further, increasing transpiration demand while soil water is already limited. This is a climate–water–vegetation feedback. Restoration that plants water-demanding exotic trees in such settings can fail and waste scarce moisture.

Drought-tolerant native grasses, shrubs, and trees, plus mulch and microcatchments, are more consistent with dryland ecology. Water-harvesting contours, bunds, and residue cover are water-retention strategies, not decorative landscaping.

4. Aquatic and soil-dwelling organisms
Even in farmland, temporary ponds, drainage lines, and moist soil layers support amphibians, soil mesofauna, and microbial processes. When soil moisture is chronically low, these microhabitats collapse. Pollinator foraging can also decline if flowering plants fail under drought.

5. Metrics affected by water-retention interventions
- Soil moisture: expected direction upward if infiltration improves and evaporation falls.
- Plant diversity: upward if drought-adapted natives establish.
- Species survival: reduced mortality risk for drought-sensitive taxa only if habitat and water both improve.
- Climate stress at the soil surface: downward with shade and cover.

Time horizon: moisture benefits from mulch and cover can appear in the first wet season. Vegetation structural recovery is multi-year. This document does not license invented percentage yield or species-count gains.

6. Source basis
Synthesis consistent with IPCC AR6 dryland and drought discussions, UNCCD land degradation framing, and FAO dryland agriculture guidance on soil cover and water harvesting. Darukaa page numbers refer to this document.
